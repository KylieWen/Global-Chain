Report Highlights:   
Brazil’s soybean sector is projected to grow in 2025/26, with post revising the total projected planted 
area to 49.1 million hectares and estimated production reaching 176 million metric tons in 2026, driven 
by favorable weather and technological advances. Despite record yields, financial challenges persist in 
2025, due to high input costs and rising interest rates. Export projections increased to 114 million metric 
tons for MY 2025/26, supported by expanded port capacity and growing demand from China. Domestic 
soybean processing is also expected to rise, fueled by livestock feed demand and biodiesel mandates, 
strengthening Brazil’s position in both international and internal markets.

BRAZIL’S SOYBEAN AREA, PRODUCTION AND YIELD ESTIMATES (MY 2024/25) 
Area 
Post revises up its Marketing Year (MY) 2024/25 area estimate to 47.6 million hectares (ha), an increase 
of 300 thousand ha. This represents a slight increase of 0.6 percent compared to the previous report. This 
expansion would represent a total increase of three percent, compared to the USDA official number of 
46.15 million ha for MY 2023/24. Over the past few months, several forecasting agencies have reported 
the identification of new soybean areas through the usage of geoprocessing technologies and satellite 
imagery, projecting a significant increase in total area for MY 2025/26.  
 
Production 
Post maintains its original production projection for MY 2024/25 at 169.5 million metric tons (MMT). 
Despite the delayed start to planting due to late rainfall in the Central-West region, crop development 
proceeded as early as expected, supported by broadly favorable weather conditions. Agrometeorological 
monitoring systems have reported favorable weather for most parts of Brazil during the first half of 
2025, contributing to a production recovery from last year, when Brazil faced difficulties due to weather 
instability caused by the El Niño climate patterns. Harvest losses have been mainly concentrated in the 
southern part of Mato Grosso do Sul, the western region of Paraná, and more severely in Rio Grande do 
Sul, a state facing its fourth consecutive crop failure.  
 
Yield 
Favorable weather conditions in most parts of the country during the first half of 2025 were one of the 
contributing factors that led to recovery in productivity in 2025 and a 6 percent increase in yield 
compared to 2024. This performance has resulted in a revised average yield of 3.56 metric tons per 
hectare (mt/ha) for MY 2024/25, slightly below our original estimate of 3.58 mt/ha, but still a record-
breaking result overall. 
MY 2024/25 was the most productive year for soybean production in the history of Brazil, and though 
the state of Goiás was the most productive state, with an average yield of 4.12 mt/ha, the most 
productive region has been the Southeast region of Brazil, with an average yield of 3.97 mt/ha. 
The MATOPIBA region, a promising region for the future of soybean production in Brazil, comprising 
the states of Maranhão, Tocantins, Piauí, and Bahia, had an average yield of 3.74 mt/ha, with Bahia 
being the most productive state in the region with an average of 4.08 mt/ha. 
In Mato Grosso, the state with the highest soybean production volume, average productivity was 3.90 
mt/ha, making it the 5th most productive state in the country. The state with the lowest level of 
productivity was Rio Grande do Sul, with recent yield numbers at 2.08 mt/ha.

New Guidelines and the Updated 'Vazio Sanitário' Calendar for Plant Disease Control 
In 2024, Brazil’s Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MAPA) established new rules and guidelines 
for controlling Asian soybean rust, pests, and other general plant diseases through Ordinance No. 1,124. 
The regulation updates the rules for managing Asian soybean rust, details record-keeping obligations 
and deadlines, broadens the definition of executing agencies, strengthens requirements for exceptional 
cultivation, and tightens restrictions on soybean-on-soybean planting.

BRAZIL’S SOYBEAN AREA, PRODUCTION AND YIELD PROJECTIONS (MY 2025/26) 
Area 
Post estimates that for MY 2025/26, soybean planted area will increase by 3 percent compared to the 
new MY 2024/25 estimates, reaching a total of 49.1 million ha. Post contacts suggest that with the end 
of, or more flexibility in, the Soy Moratorium, a significant expansion of soybean planted area could be 
reported in 2026.  
The Soy Moratorium is an agreement established in 2006 by major soybean traders, environmental 
organizations, and the Brazilian government to prevent the sale of soybeans grown on land deforested in 
the Amazon biome after July 2008. The goal is to control deforestation driven by soybean expansion.  
Recently, various stakeholders—including agribusiness associations, members of the Agricultural 
Caucus (Bancada Ruralista), and certain trade companies—have advocated for the termination or 
relaxation of the Soy Moratorium. They argue that the policy unjustly restricts economic development in 
the Amazon region and contravenes Brazilian legislation on the issue. 
Although the legitimacy of the Soy Moratorium and its benefits to the agricultural sector were 
recognized by the Supreme Court of Brazil, the court later revised part of its injunction and authorized 
the state of Mato Grosso to begin withholding tax incentives from companies participating in the 
moratorium, starting in January 2026, which could hinder the future of the agreement.  
Additional details on the Brazilian Soy Moratorium can be found in the recently posted GAIN report: 
Brazil's Soy Moratorium - Balancing Economic Interests and Regulatory Measures | BR2025-0016. 
 
Production 
Post revises up its MY 2025/26 soybean production projection to 176 MMT. This represents a nearly 2 
percent increase from Post’s initial projection of 173 MMT. This increase is mainly driven by the 
expansion of soybean planted area, supported by high global demand, stable weather with an El Niño 
season, and an increase in productivity compared to the prior and current years. 
 
Yield  
Productivity is projected to increase, mainly due to more favorable weather conditions compared to the 
recent El Niño climate season, which also carried aftermath weather characteristics into the current year. 
Innovation in irrigation systems, availability of new technologies, and the ‘vazio sanitário’ guidelines 
are some other factors expected to contribute to a highly productive soybean harvest year in Brazil. Post 
projects the average national yield in Brazil to reach 3.58 mt/ha during MY 2025/26.

Environmental Licensing Reform to Impact Soybean Expansion in Brazil 
Along with more flexibility with the Soy Moratorium, another factor that could potentially impact the 
soybean sector is an environmental bill currently under review. The Brazilian Senate has approved the 
General Environmental Licensing Law (PL 2.159/2021). The bill simplifies and unifies environmental 
licensing rules and now returns to the House of Representatives for further review. 
Although it does not directly affect deforestation procedures, the law is expected to unlock key 
infrastructure projects like roads, warehouses, ethanol plants, and the Ferrogrão railway, potentially 
saving billions in freight costs and boosting competitiveness. The measure also facilitates licensing for 
renewable energy sources such as solar and wind, lowering energy costs for farmers. Overall, the sector 
sees this as a step toward reduced bureaucracy, improved infrastructure, and greater legal and 
environmental security. 
Post contacts and key figures in the soybean sector estimate that the implementation of this law could 
result in BRL 8 billion in freight savings and generate over 6,000 new jobs.

Financing Constraints Could Be an Impediment to Secure Further Investments  
Despite a record grain harvest in 2025, Brazil’s agricultural sector faces financial stress due to 
unfavorable economic conditions. Though the Brazilian real has recently gained more stability, the sharp 
depreciation of the currency in the past couple of years has raised the cost of imported inputs such as 
fertilizers and agrochemicals, squeezing profit margins. Simultaneously, the sharp rise in domestic 
interest rates—from 10.5 percent to 15 percent annually over the past year—has significantly increased 
financing costs, making it more challenging and expensive for producers to refinance debt or sustain 
operations.

Trade 
Post maintains its original projection for soybean exports for MY 2024/25 at 108.3 MMT. Earlier this 
year, it was projected that Brazil could experience an increase in soybean exports, mainly driven by 
recent tariff conflicts and a potential new market strategy initiated by the People’s Republic of China 
(PRC). However, according to Post contacts and specialists in the Brazilian soybean market, a spike in 
soybean exports driven by an incentive of this nature will likely only take effect in the upcoming years. 
Based on data from the Chinese Customs Statistics, obtained through Trade Data Monitor LLC (TDM), 
PRC, the number one destination for U.S. and Brazilian soybeans, has historically imported around 19 
MMT of soybeans during the first quarter of the year for the past five years. Though this level of imports 
by the PRC has overall remained the same during the first quarter of 2025, according to the U.S. Census 
Bureau, the PRC has decreased its soybean imports from the United States by eight percent during the 
same period compared to last year. Data from the Brazilian Ministry of Development, Industry, and 
Trade shows that Brazil exported 16.9 million metric tons (MMT) of soybeans to the PRC during the 
first quarter of 2025. This represents an increase of nearly seven percent compared to the 15.8 MMT 
exported during the same period in 2024. The month of March was the second largest month of soybean 
exports to the PRC in a single month, with 11.1 MMT of soybeans exported. The highest monthly 
soybean exports from Brazil to the PRC occurred in April 2021, with 11.6 MMT exported.

Post revised its forecast of soybean exports for MY 2025/26, increasing it to 114 MMT, a 1.7 percent 
increase from the original estimate of 112 MMT. This increase is mainly driven by possible 
opportunities Brazil initiated through ongoing tariff disputes and market strategy. Another factor that 
supports an eventual increase in the number of soybean exports and the country’s capacity to 
accommodate this increase in demand is the expansion of terminal STS11 at the Port of Santos, which is 
currently operated by COFCO International, the largest Chinese food and agriculture company. 
In 2022, COFCO won the 25-year concession for the agricultural solid bulk terminal STS11 with a BRL 
10 billion bid and a commitment of more than BRL 764 million in investments at the port. Part of this 
investment will be directed toward the modernization and expansion of the terminal’s grain handling 
capacity, which will expand the dynamic capacity of the station from 3 MMT to 14.3 MMT. 
Operations at STS11 are expected to start in 2026, with the station set to be one of the largest in the Port 
of Santos, which currently accounts for nearly 30 percent of total soybean exports, indicating that this 
investment in port capacity expansion could be a needle mover in the soybean export market.

Prices 
Brazilian soybean prices declined significantly in early 2025. In May, farmgate prices were 
approximately 2 percent lower than the previous year, influenced by a recovery in the Brazilian real 
value and reduced domestic demand during this period. In April, prices ranged between BRL 122.42 and 
BRL 135.49 per 60 kg sack, varying by region.

Domestic Consumption and Processed Products 
Post increased its original projection for Brazil’s soybean crush to 57 MMT, a 2.1 percent increase from 
the previous year. This expansion reflects Brazil’s large protein production industries requiring more soy 
meal for animal feed, as well as the government’s B15 biodiesel mandate, expected to take effect 
between 2025 and 2026, which boosts demand for soy oil as a key feedstock. 
Recent internal consumption data indicate that Brazil’s soybean industry is becoming more vertically 
integrated, with greater value being added domestically rather than relying solely on raw soybean 
exports. As a result, the soybean complex (grains, meal, and oil) is playing an increasingly critical role 
in Brazil's internal economic activity, linking agriculture with energy (biodiesel) and food production 
chains.

Soybean Oil 
The current projected crush rate of 57 MMT would produce an estimated 11.5 MMT of soy oil, with 
nearly 13 percent of total production exported to other countries. 
During the first five months of 2025, Brazil exported a total of 658 thousand tons of soy oil, a nearly 30 
percent increase compared to the same period in 2024. India remains the main destination for Brazilian 
soy oil, accounting for more than 70 percent of its total exports so far in 2025.

Soybean Meal 
Brazil’s estimated crush for 2025 is projected to result in 43.5 MMT of soy meal production, with more 
than half of total production designated for exports. 
In 2024, Brazil exported over 23.1 MMT of soy meal, with 9.3 MMT exported in the first five months of 
the year. In 2025, Brazil has exported more than 9.6 MMT of soy meal, a 3 percent increase compared 
to the same period last year. 
Indonesia remains the main destination for Brazilian soy meal, followed by Thailand and Europe, 
similar destinations to previous years.
